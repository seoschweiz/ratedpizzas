Araldo Madrid: Veronese pizza in the heart of the capital

Araldo brings a style of pizza shaped in Verona to central Madrid, with long-fermented dough, Italian ingredients and a character distinct from classic Neapolitan pizza.

A Veronese identity in central Madrid

Araldo stands out in Madrid by looking north rather than south for its pizza tradition. The restaurant’s roots are in Verona, and the result is presented as a thinner, more compact pizza: soft within, crisp at the edge and designed to let the dough remain part of every bite. That distinction matters in a city where the Neapolitan model often dominates the conversation.

What guests repeatedly notice

Across the guest experiences supplied to RatedPizzas, the clearest pattern is flavor. Diners repeatedly describe the pizzas as aromatic, handmade and prepared with fresh, high-quality ingredients. Tomato-based pizzas attract particular praise, with the sauce, cheese and toppings remembered as a balanced whole rather than isolated elements.

The restaurant’s own responses reinforce that intention: toppings are meant to complement one another without masking the aroma and taste of the dough. Optional additions may be suggested for certain combinations, but they are not presented as necessary to enjoy the pizza.

San Giacomo, Salsiccia e Cipolle and Madrazo Original

Several named pizzas help make the feedback useful. San Giacomo appears as a signature choice, while Salsiccia e Cipolle and Madrazo Original 2018 are remembered for strong, lasting flavor. One visitor described the meal as memorable even a year later—an unusually clear sign that the combination of sauce, cheese and ingredients made an impression.

Official information describes a dough made with organic semi-wholemeal stone-ground flour, sourdough and a controlled fermentation of at least 60 hours. RatedPizzas has not yet tested that method in person, so we report it as the restaurant’s stated process rather than an independently verified technical judgment.

More than pizza

The supplied reviews show that the kitchen should not be reduced to pizza alone. Parmigiana and polpette receive favorable mentions, as do pasta dishes. One diner found the character of the pasta especially aromatic and even more striking than the pizza dough. These observations suggest a broader Italian restaurant experience rather than a single-product stop.

Dessert feedback is more nuanced. Pistachio tiramisu is remembered for a pronounced pistachio character, although one guest still preferred the structure of a traditional tiramisu. That distinction is useful: the dessert may appeal most to diners looking for a richer, clearly flavored variation on the classic.

Service that adds to the meal

Service is another consistent strength. Guests describe the team as fast, attentive and capable of making confident recommendations. A conversation with Iacopo after one meal was remembered almost as a short masterclass—evidence that product knowledge and personal hospitality can become part of the experience rather than simply supporting it.

An informal, genuinely Italian atmosphere

The restaurant is described as attractive, comfortable and authentically Italian without depending on formality or social-media spectacle. Even details such as the washrooms have drawn positive attention. The atmosphere appears suited to diners who prioritize good food and informed service over dressing up for the occasion.

Its Calle de los Madrazo address also makes Araldo a practical stop while exploring central Madrid. One reported bill of €55 for two people was considered very good value for the quality received, but this is a historical guest example rather than a current price promise.

RatedPizzas conclusion

The combined picture is unusually coherent: a distinctive Veronese dough, expressive tomato-based pizzas, carefully balanced toppings, worthwhile dishes beyond pizza and service with real product knowledge. The principal qualification concerns personal taste in richer desserts, not the overall cooking. Until RatedPizzas visits in person, Araldo Madrid receives no independent score—but the available evidence makes it a strong candidate for diners seeking an Italian pizzeria with a clearly defined identity in the centre of Madrid.
