Araldo Madrid: Veronese pizza in the heart of the capital

Araldo brings a style of pizza shaped in Verona to central Madrid, with long-fermented dough, Italian ingredients and a character distinct from classic Neapolitan pizza.

A different Italian pizza tradition

Madrid diners often associate Italian pizza with Naples, but Araldo offers another reference point. Its identity comes from Verona in northern Italy. The restaurant describes a thinner, more compact base designed to remain soft inside and crisp outside, giving the finished pizza a different structure from the highly inflated Neapolitan style.

Long fermentation at the center

The dough is the clearest part of Araldo’s proposition. Official restaurant information highlights organic semi-wholemeal stone-ground flour, sourdough and a controlled fermentation of at least 60 hours. These details suggest a deliberate focus on flavor, texture and digestibility rather than a generic fast-service base. RatedPizzas has not yet tested the dough in person, so this page records the restaurant’s stated method without assigning a technical score.

Italian ingredients and signature combinations

Araldo’s menu places Italian produce at the center, including San Marzano tomato, fiordilatte mozzarella, cheeses and cured meats. Signature pizzas such as San Giacomo and San Marzano help express the restaurant’s Veronese identity. The wider offer has also included starters, salads and house-made gelato, although dishes and availability should be checked against the current menu.

A central Madrid address

The Calle de los Madrazo location sits close to Madrid’s major cultural and commercial streets. Its central position makes Araldo practical for a meal between the Prado area, Gran Vía and Puerta del Sol. The interior has been presented as informal and welcoming, with Italian references and spaces suitable for both couples and groups.

Recognition without an invented ranking

Araldo’s official information points to recognition by Gambero Rosso and inclusion by 50 Top Pizza. Such distinctions provide useful context, but RatedPizzas does not convert awards or popularity into its own score. A future first-hand visit would be needed to judge fermentation, bake, ingredient balance and consistency independently.

RatedPizzas conclusion

Araldo is a valuable addition to the Madrid guide because it broadens the definition of Italian pizza in the city. Its Veronese background, extended fermentation and ingredient-led menu give diners a clear reason to compare it with Madrid’s many Neapolitan addresses. For now, this is an editorial profile based on public and official information rather than a first-hand RatedPizzas review.
